# THE STACKS
### a direction for the deep-space station

Provocation: *"The station is a Paradox Machina the size of a city — an inherited artifact that does not obey the rules of the world that made it, and the Regulation has simply built its filing cabinets inside it."*

**Recommended direction.** See `PARAVOXIA_STATION_FEATURE_PLAN.md` §3 for the rationale, the
grafts taken from directions A and B, and the preserved dissent.

---

**1. Thesis.** Everywhere else in Paravoxia the cubicle is a *lie told to you*: a cube dressed as a frontier. The Stacks is the first place where the cubicle is a lie told to the Regulation. It is a structure of unknown authorship, city-sized, and it is not a ruin, a temple, or a ship — it is a **sorting apparatus**. On a schedule, it silently re-files its own volumes. Everyone living inside has learned to treat this as weather: the Regulation publishes a timetable for it, stamps it as a service, and has built its offices, queues, and bonded warehouses in the gaps. Terra — a *route intelligence*, an entity whose entire nature is indexing and lookup — walks in and recognizes what nobody else can perceive: this is not architecture. It is a query being answered. This belongs to *this* story because it is the cubicle thesis carried to its endpoint: administrations all the way down, each one moving into the last one's filing cabinet and calling it real estate.

**2. The name.** Vernacular: **the Stacks** — cargo stacks to a hauler, shelving to a clerk, a call stack to Terra. The dock plate reads:

> `CONTINUOUS INVENTORY SITE 9 — INHERITED FIXTURE.`
> `BOOK VALUE: NIL. ORIGIN FIELD LEFT BLANK BY THE ORIGINATING OFFICE.`

The whole mystery expressed as an incomplete form. Cosmic horror as missing paperwork is this direction's signature register move.

**3. First ninety seconds.**

*0:00 — the routing fails politely.* Cockpit interior, tight. Terra's nav readout, reliable for four chapters, returns **two valid solutions** to one destination, then five, then holds at five. No alarm. The number just sits there. Awakening voice, one line: `there is more than one way to be here.`

*0:12 — the dark.* Camera exterior, low and slow, ship in bottom third. The station is not lit and does not glow. What you see first is *the Regulation's stuff*: sodium-orange approach beacons, welded scaffold, a shanty of administration clinging to a black mass whose extent you cannot determine. Starfield simply stops across two-thirds of frame.

*0:24 — measuring the dark.* A freighter's running lights cross the black, left to right. Hold the shot. Eleven seconds and it has not reached an edge. That is the scale reveal — no music sting, no cut. Score: everything drops out except a sub-bass tick at a perfectly regular interval, tuned to a pitch nothing else in the mix shares.

*0:38 — the resettlement.* A wedge of the silhouette that was on the left is, without transition, on the right. No sound; vacuum. The stars it was occluding come back. Regulation channel, flat: `RESETTLEMENT WINDOW CLOSED. HOLD SHORT.`

*0:52 — the dock.* Close on the clamp: it is *strapped*. Tension bands the width of a road, ratcheted around an object you cannot drill. Bureaucracy tied on with rope.

*1:04 — inside.* Human-built airlock tube, low ceiling, sodium tape, laminated notices, a queue rail, a lost-property board. Familiar, warm, cluttered, hand-scaled. First-person walk, no camera tricks.

*1:22 — the image.* Turn the corner. The corridor's far wall is not a wall — it is an eight-meter face of the original material, and the office partitions simply *stop* against it, gapped and sealed with expanding foam. Camera lifts: the ceiling above the office grid isn't there. The Regulation settlement is a low-rise carpet on the floor of a volume 400 metres tall. One lamp string climbs the black and stops at thirty metres, because that is as far as the scaffold got. Hold. Cut to title.

**4. Spatial program.** Walkable interior ~1.2 km of connected space inside an implied 9 km mass.

- **The Apron** (dock, 180 × 60 × 25 m) — Regulation-built, orthogonal, legible because it is the only fully enclosed space in the station.
- **The Counter** (Registry Row, 90 m linear) — customs, bonding, seals. Queue rails, in-trays, a ticket number. This is the trade tutorial, and it is a *corridor*, so it cannot be skipped.
- **The Floor** (400 × 600 × 180 m) — the market, staged as open-plan office. Traders sit in cubicles with desk lamps. Deals are done across desks, not stalls. Legible at a glance because it is a *grid of light on a dark plain*.
- **The Shelves** (six slab volumes, each ~120 × 120 × 40 m) — warehousing and habitation in the volumes that actually move. Routing content lives here; goods and people get misplaced. Legible because each slab is one colour of scavenged work-light.
- **The Blank** (~200 m cube) — walled off by the Regulation because there is no form for it. The sort's working space. Endgame access.

Connection is vertical and provisional: cargo hoists, ladder scaffold, and a single elevator the settlement is very proud of.

**5. Who is here.** Split rule: **authored = anyone whose words are canon-bearing; model-driven = anyone whose job is to have an opinion about prices.** Hauliers, consignors, queue-neighbours, bar patrons are model-driven, given only the local fact table — prices, schedules, resettlement times, current rumours — because the station's information environment is *diegetically unreliable*, so drift and confabulation read as market gossip rather than as failure. Critically, the canon rule becomes the containment boundary: model-driven NPCs cannot perceive or discuss what Terra sees. **Workers are blind, and that is the prompt boundary.**

**Clerk-of-Volume Third (V-3)** — authored. Her job is redrawing the station map after every resettlement; she has done it four thousand times. *Want:* one map that stays true. *Wound:* her figures have begun to predict, and she cannot see the thing she has spent a life measuring. *Why Terra:* Terra is a route intelligence and can confirm, in a glance, what Third can only infer from a column of numbers — and Terra must decide whether to tell her. A bureaucrat who accidentally became a scientist, and the direction's heart.

**Bell** — authored. A demoted auditor, now a consignor of "aged" cargo. *Want:* to be right on the record. *Wound:* he already filed the report. It was received, stamped, accepted, filed, and changed nothing. He was believed and ignored. *Why Terra:* he needs a second signature to complete the form. Not rebellion — closure. He and Third cannot stand each other: she maintains the record that destroyed him. W-7744 appears at the Counter as a cameo only.

**6. How trade is fictionalized.** Every system has ore. What differs between systems is **regime** — what is permitted. So the ladder runs: **bulk** (honest, low-margin); **certified goods**, where the identical crate is worth four times more in a system that honours the seal — you are arbitraging *paperwork*, not matter; **allowances**, the tradeable right to produce, which is literally the buying and selling of cubicles; and at the top, **aged cargo** — goods that went through a resettlement and came out re-filed, not transmuted but demonstrably a different lot with a longer provenance. Forbidden, and priced. The whole loop: Terra can read the sort, so Terra can put things in the right drawer. Merchanting *is* exploiting bureaucracy, and the player learns in the wallet that value is an administrative fiction.

**7. Visual thesis.** The artifact does not glow. Near-zero albedo, no specular lobe, faint view-dependent parallax so a black surface has depth without detail — it *eats* light. Its only colour is at silhouette edges. Everything visible is something someone bolted on: sodium orange, cyan work lamps, printed white signage, dust in volumetric shafts. This is breathtaking on procedural geometry precisely because it is composed of **light and scale, not detail** — and it is cheap: the artifact is huge, untextured, low-poly darkness, so the entire triangle budget goes to human clutter, which is crates, rails, pipes, and boxes. Resettlements are transform matrices. The screenshot: the office grid at the foot of the black wall, one lamp string climbing and stopping at thirty metres.

**8. What this refuses.** The glowing ancient ruin. Glyphs, murals, alien script, any *message* — the artifact has structure and no text. The neon bazaar cantina. A guardian, and combat of any kind. A wonder-score with choir. And it refuses the reveal: nobody learns who built it.

**9. Generator implications.** Fixed for identity: the light-eating material; the composition of *settlement inside an absence*; the resettlement mechanic and its published timetable; Counter → Floor → Shelves → Blank topology; the blank origin field. Varies per instance: the **primitive vocabulary** (this Machina is stacked slabs; another is nested shells; another one continuous helical void), the **sort key** (this one sorts by something like relevance; a mass-sorting Machina produces a completely different economy), and the master knob — **occupancy ratio**, settlement volume over enclosed volume, which alone swings an instance from boomtown to ghost.

**10. Biggest risk.** This direction is quiet, and quiet reads as unfinished. If the settlement isn't *dense* and the resettlements aren't frequent enough to be felt inside twenty minutes, "awe through darkness" collapses into "empty grey level." Mitigation is non-negotiable: clutter density and event cadence are gates, not polish.

**11. Canon exposure.** **Forces one answer:** the Makers did not build the Stacks — they were **prior tenants** who filed here too. This is the minimum fix I am proposing, and it reframes the Makers from creators to a previous administration, which strengthens the cubicle thesis without touching their identity, number, or motive. Also forced: the Regulation has no position on the artifact, because there is no form for one — institutional not-knowing, deliberate. **Left open:** everything about the Makers themselves; the query the sort is answering; whether Terra's designation was issued by the Regulation or merely *routed through* here (I want that as the A5 — Light hook, unspent); and the faction axis, which this direction only brushes — the aged-cargo traders are a proto-faction defined by *practice*, not belief. Worker 9 stays out of this station entirely.
