# Blind first-time read — 2026-07-28

Reader: `story-naive-reader`, opus. No tools, no repository access. The entire
40-beat intent-free corpus was inlined in its prompt; it saw no Bible, no plan,
no treatment, no code, no comment, and no mention that a space station exists
anywhere in this project.

Two earlier attempts were discarded. One gave the reader file paths instead of
inlined text; having no tools, it fabricated an entire game — fidelity bars,
eleven chairs, a buried station — formatted to look like a file read, with
`tool_uses: 0`. The invention was thematically plausible, which is what made it
dangerous. Verified this run by spot-checking a dozen quoted lines against the
committed corpus.

## Headline

**It deduced the hidden pillar from the prologue.** "I'm a route intelligence
called TERRA," sourced to the crawl's `ROUTING ADDENDUM · FOR TERRA ONLY`. Design
intent is deducible by end of ch1; this reader had it before gameplay began.
Route to the alignment judge — that is either the design working or leaking early,
and it is not the orchestrator's call.

**Asked what it wanted next, it did not reach for a place. It reached for
consequence.** Verbatim: *"yes, I'd keep playing — but not for more building. I
want the Authority to answer the refusal; I want to know whether Worker 9 is
alive and whether the name took."* And: *"What would have changed my 'yes' into a
'hell yes' is one line of consequence in the final beat — a carrier tone, a
second silhouette on the ridge, anything that says the world I left knows what I
did."*

This matters for sequencing. The Chapter Director's departure motive is
logistical — a bonded cell the habitat needs. The blind reader explicitly does
not want more building. The two are not the same need.

## Where it drags

- **Chapter 1, eight beats, one verb.** `HOLD [E]` across fixed → track → raster
  → depth → nav → iso → lift → anomaly, in monochrome. The idea (perspective
  issued as equipment) is called superb; the play is not. `ch1-track` is *"not a
  beat; it's a loading screen with a caption on it."*
- **`ch3-dusk`** — 45 seconds standing still for one line.
- **`ch7-reconstruct`** — five fetch-and-return build steps immediately after the
  refusal, the story's peak, carrying one caption. *"Ten minutes of inventory
  management with a haiku taped to it."*
- **`ch8-launch`** — no lines at all. *"That silence reads as an omission, not a
  choice."*
- **`ch9-settle`** — seven objectives; good lines *"doing PR for a checklist."*

## Protect

The crawl (`CLAUSE 4. THERE IS NO CLAUSE 4.`); the voyage cards, especially MASS
AUDIT and ILLUMINATION; the naming interstitial (`(retained.)`) — called the best
beat in the game; `WORKER, THIS IS NOT YOUR FAULT. (UNPRECEDENTED MESSAGE)`; the
label degrading to `THERE IS NOTHING HE`; the `VIOLATION:` stack collapsing to
`wor ker`; the eight star lines in `ch4-vigil` — *"the only slow beat that earned
its length"*; the refusal.

## Independently confirms a runtime finding

The reader wanted *"to refuse earlier… I wanted the refusal to be mine, not
scheduled."* It felt as a player exactly what the code shows: `ch4-comply`
advances to `ch4-defy` unconditionally, so dousing your own fire is compulsory
before refusal is offered. A structural fact and a felt complaint, arrived at
independently.

## Wanted and could not

Reply to the Authority — *"the one act the fiction forbids is the one act I spent
the whole game wanting."* Find Worker 9 — *"I named it. Then the name
evaporates."* Verify the constellations. Go back.

## Its three changes, in its priority order

1. Cut Chapter 1 by half.
2. Pay off the name and the reply.
3. Give ch7–9 a voice and a spine, or compress the crafting and spend the budget
   on lines.
