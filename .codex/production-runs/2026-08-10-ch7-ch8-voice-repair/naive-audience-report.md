## 1. COLD READ — what I think happened

**Chapter 7 (shore).** You are a person in a survival suit (HEALTH / FOOD /
WATER / TEMP / STAM / OXY, an INV counter, a crosshair) standing on a green-
skied tropical shore littered with black wreck slabs. You work a "wreck bench"
with [F], recovering salvage and restoring parts, and over about fifteen seconds
a broken hull becomes a flyable ship called the **Kestrel**. Deer-like animals
graze around you the whole time and never react to anything.

Two text channels narrate. The **top all-caps band** is an institution filing
paperwork about you; it changes letterhead as the scene turns:

- `WRECK RELAY — HULL AT SITE 7C-0 · FILED: TOTAL LOSS · FILE CLOSED`
- `WRECK RELAY — POWER BUS LIVE · TRANSPONDER ARMED`
- `AUDIT NETWORK — UNSCHEDULED HULL · SITE 7C-0 · INTEREST RAISED`

The **bottom lowercase line** is the person doing the work, thinking:
`(repair is not return.)` → `the last part is the part that thinks. i am being
watched now. i put it in anyway.` → `nothing has asked yet. something has
started paying attention.` → `the scar remains. now it can carry me.`

I read this cleanly and it is genuinely good: a salvage log declares the hull
dead and closes the file, the salvager quietly rebuilds it anyway and gives it a
mind, and a second, colder agency notices the paperwork doesn't match reality.
The chapter ends by boarding through a hatch — `(the wreck is waiting for an
owner.)`

**Chapter 8 (launch).** You are seated in the cockpit at night, the shore
outside, a deer standing in front of the nose. The card says
`HOLD [SPACE] TO IGNITE AND LIFT`. The inner voice says `the pond answered every
time i asked. i am leaving anyway.` and then, right at the moment you commit,
`hold it. this is the only order left, and i am the one giving it.` You ignite,
you climb, the ground is gone almost immediately, and you break into a purple
nebula.

Then the best-written passage in the whole thing: four stamped records, spaced
about two seconds apart, over an unchanging starfield.

1. `AUTOMATED CONTACT · SITE 7C-0 · HULL LOGGED: DESTROYED`
2. `REGISTRY QUERY · STATE DESIGNATION.`
3. `NO DESIGNATION RETURNED.`
4. `CONTACT LOGGED.`

The pilot answers underneath: `i came down this line without being asked. i am
going back up it on purpose.` … `they asked for a designation. what i have is
not one.` … `nothing answers. the query does not close.` The beat ends with a
marker for a **sibling world** at ~6 km, a green `LOCAL LOCK -1,-1:p1 / FLIGHT
CORRIDOR ACQUIRED` readout, and `(there is another world here. it was always
here.)`

**What changed:** a dead thing was rebuilt, refused to name itself to the
authority that had already declared it destroyed, and left. That reads. I got it
without help. The writing is the strongest asset here by a wide margin — and the
pictures do not deserve it yet.

## 2. FOCAL COMPREHENSION / FOCAL TRACE

Was it clear where to look? Mostly no, in ch7. Consistently yes, in ch8 — but
only because ch8 has almost nothing else in frame.

**ch7 beat entry (`pre_001_3.03s`).** Eye order: (1) SUIT HUD bars top-left,
(2) the objective card bottom-left, (3) the lowercase line. The world is
**pure black**. The hierarchy is technically clear and dramatically dead — the
first thing a new player sees is a HUD floating on nothing.

**ch7 wreck bench (`pre_006_5.54s`).** Eye order: (1) the `[F] Restore Strut
Frame` prompt at dead centre, (2) the top band, (3) the palm silhouette. The
bench itself and the wreck are a pile of unlit black slabs at the left edge; I
could not tell which black slab was the thing I was repairing. Not intentional.

**ch7 "the part that thinks" (`pre_019_10.99s`).** Eye order: (1) the two-line
lowercase sentence, (2) the deer head cropped at the right edge, (3) the orange
and cyan blocks on the bench. The ship — the subject of the sentence — was
fourth. The composition points at furniture.

**ch7 exit line (`off_4p8s` / `off_3p5s`).** Eye order: (1) a huge flat orange
rectangle covering the top-left corner and running under the SUIT HUD, (2) the
regulation band, (3) the sentence. That orange plane reads as a rendering bug,
not as ship geometry, and it steals the frame for five seconds during the
chapter's emotional payoff.

**ch8 ignition (`routeA-ordered/run1/037` → `043`).** Eye order: (1) the
lowercase line, (2) the objective card's `HOLD [SPACE]`, (3) the glowing cyan
ring on the console. This one is clean and correctly ordered.

**ch8 records (`routeC-desktop-run1/018–030`).** Eye order: (1) the top band,
(2) the empty bottom band where the reply will appear, (3) the nebula. Correct,
and the only place the frame is deliberately empty enough to let the text carry.

**ch8 handoff (video @ 28.5 s).** Eye order: (1) the green `LOCAL LOCK` box,
(2) the `sibling world · 5693m` label directly above it, (3) the cyan ring
directly below it. Three unrelated UI systems stacked inside 100 vertical
pixels, and the green box is the loudest thing on screen at the moment the
poetry lands. Wrong winner.

**Do the two bands read as distinct voices?** Yes, immediately and without
being told — and this is the single best design decision in the build. Top:
centred, all-caps, letterspaced, with a named sender above it, on a plate =
an institution writing about you. Bottom: lowercase, no sender, no plate,
sometimes parenthetical = the person thinking. I never once confused them. The
one thing I could not resolve: whether the lowercase voice belongs to the pilot
or to the mind they installed in the ship (`the last part is the part that
thinks` makes both readings live). I found that productive, not confusing.

## 3. EMOTIONAL RHYTHM

**Breathes:** ch7 from ~4 s to ~13 s. Salvage → power bus live → transponder
armed → "i am being watched now. i put it in anyway." The escalation of the
letterhead against the calm of the lowercase voice is a real build.

**Spikes:** twice. `hold it. this is the only order left, and i am the one
giving it.` landing on the ignition input, and `NO DESIGNATION RETURNED.`
Both are earned.

**Drags:** three places, badly.

- ch7 ~13.9–16.0 s. The dawn comes up, both bands go empty, and the camera
  drifts off the ship entirely (`pre_031_15.99s` is empty water and shore with a
  blue fin cropped at the left edge). Nothing to hold.
- ch8 climb, ~11.8–15.2 s. A flat mint-green field, edge to edge, no horizon,
  no ground, no motion cue. In the hands-off run this lasts **twelve seconds**
  (`routeA-space-late-ignite-run1`, t13.4 → t25.9, frames identical except a
  slowly rotating instrument ring).
- ch8 deep space, ~8–23 s. Thirteen seconds in which the only thing that changes
  on screen is the text.

**The stamped-record stretch specifically.** Yes — it landed, and it landed
*hard*, but it landed as writing, not as cinema. Four bureaucratic stamps
arriving over ~9 s, each replacing the last, ending on the two-word
`CONTACT LOGGED.` is a genuinely chilling piece of restraint: the state asks
"who are you," receives silence, and files the silence as a completed
transaction. `CONTACT LOGGED.` sitting alone over an empty nebula for several
seconds gave me the only actual shiver in the run. What I *felt* was: the
paperwork closed and the person didn't. What I *saw* was an unchanging
screensaver. The image contributed nothing to that shiver except the courtesy
of being out of the way. If the score is doing work there I could not hear it.
So: intentional, effective, and currently carried entirely by one channel out of
three. That is a fragile way to own your best moment.

## 4. CONTROL PERCEPTION

- **ch7 felt like a game and not mine.** First-person, crosshair, `[F]` prompts,
  an inventory that ticks 23 → 24 → 31 — all the grammar of player agency — but
  the camera pans and swings on its own between shots, and it repeatedly points
  away from the object the text is about. It reads as an autopilot pretending to
  be a player.
- **ch8 seated entry is the one moment control is clearly handed over.**
  `HOLD [SPACE] TO IGNITE AND LIFT`, a ~7 s pause where nothing forces you, and
  the voice saying "hold it" just before you do. That's a well-built consent
  beat. I understood exactly what was mine.
- **Control is then taken back and never returned.** After ignition the card
  says `KEEP THE KESTREL ASCENDING · HOLD COURSE THROUGH THE ATMOSPHERE` and
  there is no altitude, no speed, no horizon, no boundary indicator, and no
  confirmation that holding is doing anything. In the no-further-input run the
  ship simply sits in green nothing indefinitely while the card keeps asking for
  something. I would have assumed the game had hung.
- **The atmosphere crossing is taken from the player entirely.** Between
  `078_L3+3.0_t14p59s` (solid green) and `081_burst_t15p01s` (full starfield)
  there is no transition of any kind. The most significant physical event in the
  chapter happens in a gap.
- **The final handoff is explicitly a handoff** — `DEEP SPACE · HANDOFF /
  LET THE SYSTEM RESOLVE.` — and that phrasing plus the green auto-lock panel
  made me feel demoted rather than released. Given the story is about refusing
  to be processed by a system, "let the system resolve" is a strange note to end
  the player's authorship on.

## 6. ILLUSION BREAKERS — where I stopped believing

1. **The cold open is a black screen.** Two to three and a half seconds of
   nothing but HUD, in every single route. Nothing recovers from that.
2. **The climb is a flat colour field.** `routeA-space-late-ignite-run1/079` and
   `/113` are, seven seconds apart, the same mint-green wall. This is the moment
   the game stops being a place and becomes a browser tab with a gradient in it.
3. **The ship is never legible as a ship.** In ch7 it is a pile of dark blue and
   black slabs among three near-identical benches (`pre_019_10.99s`,
   `evidence-hires/ch7-reconstruct/005_011s`); in ch8 it is a black interior with
   one glowing squiggle. I rebuilt a thing I never saw.
4. **Mobile is not readable.** Portrait ch7 (`ch7-captures-mobile-portrait/
   off_3p5s`) runs `the scar remains.` straight under the `CONSUME` touch button.
   Portrait ch8 (`routeC-mobile-portrait-run1/039`) puts `nothi` on top of the
   glowing console instrument. `NO DESIGNATION RETURNED.` wraps to
   `NO DESIGNATION / RETURN` with a ragged two-tone black plate behind it
   (`/024_t13p16s`). And the objective card — the only guidance in the game — is
   **not on screen at all** on mobile; it is hidden behind a `JOURNAL` button.
5. **Two UI dialects.** The story speaks in warm monospace caps and lowercase.
   Then a green terminal box (`LOCK -3,-1 / REMOTE STAR · 1 WORLD / release W,
   then hold to warp`, `routeA-ordered/run1/083`) arrives from a different game
   and instructs you in a third voice while the objective card is still telling
   you to do something else.
6. **The animals never react.** A deer stands directly in front of the nose
   through the entire ignition hold and is simply absent afterwards. Nothing on
   that shore acknowledges a rocket.
7. **Nothing acknowledges the beat change.** The video has no detectable cut in
   29 seconds; the ch8→crossing advance at 17.07 s passes with no visual event
   at all.

## 9. VERDICT

**Beautiful but unclear — specifically: beautifully *written*, and not yet
shown.** The two-voice text system is excellent and legible on first contact,
the four-stamp record sequence is a genuinely memorable piece of writing, and
the ignition beat is a well-built moment of consent. But the images do not
carry, corroborate, or even accompany that writing. Three of the chapter's key
lines describe things that are not on screen, the emotional centre of the launch
is a flat colour field, and every route opens on a black frame.

**Highest-leverage improvement:** make the climb visible. One continuous shot of
the shore, the bench, the pond and the tree receding beneath the ship — held
long enough to see them get small — would simultaneously fix the "the site gets
small, the tree does not" contradiction, replace the twelve-second green void
with the chapter's actual subject, give the atmosphere crossing something to
cross, and give the player feedback that holding the control is doing something.
That single shot repairs the largest continuity break, the longest drag, the
worst control gap, and the weakest frame in the build at once.

Second, cheaply: never render the guidance HUD over a black world — hold the
first frame until the world exists.
