## 1. THE STORY AS I UNDERSTAND IT

I am W-7743. That's not a name, it's an inventory number, and the game never lets me forget it.

I'm a worker stranded at a crash site — "SITE 7C-θ" — on a world that a bureaucracy I belong to considers a loss. Before this selection begins, I've apparently been living here: a fire, a pond, a tree. The system that owns me doesn't want that. It wants a *floor*: no production, no consumption, no observation. Sleep on schedule. My inner voice — lowercase, unpunctuated, tender — answers the all-caps orders with something like private ownership: "they schedule the dark now. last night the dark was mine."

That contrast is the whole engine of the piece. Capitals are the world telling me what is real. Lowercase is me noticing that it isn't.

An auditor arrives — W-7744, a number one higher than mine, which I read as *my near-identical sibling, a version of me that never stopped complying*. He "walks like the feed looks. straight lines." For one blink I see through his eyes and recognize his world: "slabs. flat light. i lived there." So he's not an alien authority. He's my past.

He conducts an audit. He names my fire "HEAT SOURCE: PERSISTENT WITHOUT AUTHORIZATION," names the animals "BIOLOGICAL NOISE... MISCLASSIFIED AS SCENERY," and names the tree "PRIMARY ORGANIC ANOMALY." Then he makes me erase my own life. I douse my own fire — "(the warmth goes out because a voice named it wrong.)" That parenthetical is the best line in the corpus, and it does the thesis in one breath: reality here is whatever gets successfully *named*, and the audit's power is naming, not force.

Then he orders me to cut the tree with the Maw, and I refuse. The refusal is written as two of the smallest words available: "refuse. / no." And the world responds physically — "(the world exhales where it had been holding still.) (water answers first. then wings. then everything at once.)" The audit network logs the failure not as *worker disobedience* but as "MODEL REFUSED BY OBSERVATION." That's the sharpest idea in the piece: the auditor's model of reality was falsified by being looked at, and *he* is the fault, not me.

He flees and drops his field pack. I recover the Maw — his sterilization tool — and repair it. The game then deliberately withholds an instruction: "WAIT. LISTEN BEFORE ASSIGNING IT A PURPOSE," then "TEST THE MAW ON STONE... OR REMAIN WITH IT AND [F] LOWER IT TO LISTEN." Whichever I do, the narration says the same thing three ways: "(the world supplies no order.) ... direction was yours." So: the tool is neutral; authorship transferred to me. The pond "answers in structure, not instruction" — the world responds, but never commands.

The Maw marks something underwater. I dive, and come back "with breath. Returned with a way to leave." I rebuild the wreck into a ship, the Kestrel. The registry files the hull as TOTAL LOSS, FILE CLOSED — and then, when I fly it, "UNSCHEDULED HULL · INTEREST RAISED." I'm now an anomaly in someone's ledger. "nothing has asked yet. something has started paying attention."

I leave. Automated contact asks for a designation; I don't give one; "the query does not close." I make landfall on a second world, Tidegarden — "(there is another world here. it was always here.)" I build a habitat: choose a site, craft a core, lay a foundation, enclose it, certify it, and light a second hearth. The framing is explicitly anti-colonial: "abundance is not permission"; the home is "not a claim on the world. an address inside a relationship." "HABITAT CORE ONLINE · ADDRESS NOT YET PROVEN."

Then the twist that makes the whole thing a story rather than a fantasy: the hearth fails. The core runs on a "BONDED CELL," and "(a world gives stone, water, wood. it does not give this. this was issued.)" My fabricator can't make one: "PATTERN NOT HELD · CLASS: ISSUED COMPONENT." Self-sufficiency has a hard floor, and the floor is manufactured by the thing I fled.

So I go back. I fly to the wreck relay, request a source, and get a bearing — "issued, not offered. the going is still yours." I fly to the issuing station, and at the counter I do the thing I refused to do at the automated contact: I present a designation. "DESIGNATION: W-7743 · RECORD ACCEPTED." And the narration cuts me: "(the suit is accepted before you are.)"

Inside, there's warmth I didn't make and a person — Bell, B-7073, a certified components trader — who talks in lowercase like *me*, not in capitals like the audit. She sells me the cell and explains the bond: "the cell carries an issuance record. break the record and the core will not take it. ... the seal follows the designation the counter recorded." Which is to say: the warmth of my free home is cryptographically tied to my inventory number. My independence is licensed.

I carry it back — "(the first thing a world could not give fits beneath one arm.)" — and install it. The corpus ends there.

**My read, stated as a read:** this is a story about escaping a system and then discovering that escape is a supply chain. The first half earns the fantasy of refusal; the second half taxes it. "Home is the distance you can keep alive" is the thesis line, and the bonded cell is the bill. My guess is that the game is setting up the *next* problem: the seal is a leash, Bell is either an ally or an unwitting node of the same registry, and "ADDRESS NOT YET PROVEN" is a promise that proving it will cost something. I'd also guess W-7744 comes back, since he's alive, humiliated, and logged as a *fault* — but the corpus gives me zero evidence for that beyond his survival.

## 2. REAL-TIME INTERPRETATION LOG

**Before the visitor / arrival.** Immediately hooked. The two-register voice does more characterization in four lines than most games manage in an hour. "last night the dark was mine" told me the stakes (interiority vs. schedule) without a word of exposition. When W-7744 arrived one digit away from me and I "borrowed his seeing," I assumed — and still assume — deliberate mirroring. I expected the story to go for a confrontation between two versions of the same person.

**Inspection.** I read this as a horror set-piece and it works: the escalation from fire → animals → tree is clean, and each euphemism ("scenery," "anomaly") is worse than the last. I was already anticipating the tree order.

**Compliance.** This is where the corpus was strongest and where I most wanted more. I *complied*. I doused my own fire and "Resolve[d] Organic Samples" — a phrase I read, with a lurch, as *the animals*. The game made me do the atrocity in the euphemism's own vocabulary. Then "VERIFIED REALITY FLOOR RESTORED." Genuinely excellent. But — see Coherence #2 — I could not tell from the shipped text whether I *had* to comply or merely did.

**Refusal.** The single-word refusal landed. The world's physical answer landed harder. "MODEL REFUSED BY OBSERVATION" re-colored everything before it: the audit wasn't enforcement, it was *reality maintenance*, and I broke it by being a witness.

**The Maw.** Surprised me, in a good way, by being an anti-tutorial. "WAIT. LISTEN BEFORE ASSIGNING IT A PURPOSE" is a bold instruction. But it's still an instruction, in capitals, in the same voice slot that the audit used — see Coherence #1. That undercut it for me in the moment: I was being *told* to be free.

**Dive / rebuild / launch.** Momentum. "repair is not return" and "i came down this line without being asked. i am going back up it on purpose" are strong. The registry lines (FILE CLOSED → INTEREST RAISED) built dread economically. "the query does not close" is a great, quiet horror beat.

**Tidegarden.** Here I sagged. Ten consecutive marker/instruction blocks — scan, attend, choose, craft, place, install, enclose, certify, wait, rest — with only three narration lines between them. After a first act about refusing to be directed, I spent the second act following markers. The prose ("an address inside a relationship") is arguing for autonomy while the verbs are pure checklist.

**The hearth fault.** This *rescued* the sag and re-colored it. Suddenly the checklist reads as hubris: I built a home out of found materials and one component I didn't understand. "FABRICATION IS NOT AUTHORIZED" is a beautifully placed knife. Best structural move in the corpus.

**Return / counter / Bell.** The counter scene is the emotional peak for me — presenting the designation is a real defeat, and "(the suit is accepted before you are.)" names it perfectly. Bell was the biggest surprise: the first other person who *isn't* a voice from a display. And the corpus omits her greeting, which is maddening (see Loose Threads).

**Install.** Anticlimax, partly because the corpus declares the final line absent.

## 3. COHERENCE PROBLEMS

**SEVERITY 1 — The liberation is delivered in the imperative voice of the oppressor.**
The audit speaks in all-caps commands: `REMAIN AT THE WRECK. DO NOT PRODUCE.` The post-refusal freedom speaks in *the same register, same formatting, same slot*: `WAIT. LISTEN BEFORE ASSIGNING IT A PURPOSE.` and `REMAIN WITH THE RESONANCE. / LET THE STRUCTURE SETTLE.` and `[F] CHOOSE THIS DRY, LEVEL SITE.`

That last one is the clearest failure: a marker literally named `RECOMMENDED HABITAT SITE · CHOOSE` while the narration insists "abundance is not permission... a question with many answers." I am being *recommended* the answer and then congratulated for choosing. The narration's claim — "(the world supplies no order. direction was yours.)" — is directly contradicted by the fact that a standing instruction told me to test the Maw or lower it, both options enumerated. If direction was mine, the HUD should have gone quiet. It goes quiet exactly twice ("The standing instruction clears") and both times it's the strongest the piece feels.

**SEVERITY 2 — I cannot tell whether compliance was mandatory, and that determines the entire meaning of the refusal.**
`Douse the Fire` and `Resolve Organic Samples` are presented identically to `Test Maw on Protected Tree` — the one I refused. If the first two were also refusable, then I chose to kill the animals and the story never acknowledges it. If they weren't, then the "refusal" is scripted and my agency is theatre. The narration hedges: "(the warmth goes out because a voice named it wrong.)" is passive — *the warmth goes out*, not *I put it out*. That's a dodge at the exact moment the story needs to be precise about my complicity. As shipped, I finished the scene not knowing what I had done.

**SEVERITY 3 — "Resolve Organic Samples" is the most important act in the sequence and gets zero narration.**
The fire gets a line. The tree gets a refusal. The animals — previously established as "BIOLOGICAL NOISE... MISCLASSIFIED AS SCENERY," i.e. living things I'd been sharing the site with — get one euphemistic button label and then `VERIFIED REALITY FLOOR RESTORED.` Either I killed them or I didn't; either way this is the moment that should cost most and it costs nothing.

**SEVERITY 4 — W-7744 vanishes with no consequence and no closure.**
He flees. `FAULT: W-7744 / MODEL REFUSED BY OBSERVATION` is logged. He is never mentioned again across a launch, a crossing, a colonization, a return trip, a station visit, and a second crossing. I flew *back to his employer's station*, presented my designation to their counter, and nobody mentioned the auditor who fled a site and abandoned his field pack. The registry notices an "UNSCHEDULED HULL" but not a lost auditor. That's an institution that tracks objects more carefully than people, which is thematically defensible — but the corpus never lets me see that irony land, so it reads as a dropped thread rather than a point.

**SEVERITY 5 — The stakes of the hearth fault are never quantified.**
"CONDITION: DEGRADING." Degrading toward what, by when? "(cold is coming through a wall you sealed yourself.)" is atmospheric but not a stake. I never learn whether I die, whether Tidegarden becomes uninhabitable, or whether I just lose a nice fire. Without a clock or a consequence, the whole return journey — the emotional core of act two — is motivated by mild discomfort.

**SEVERITY 6 — The bonded cell's threat is asserted but never tested.**
Bell explains the bond twice — "break the record and the core will not take it," "the seal follows the designation the counter recorded" — which is heavy foreshadowing for a betrayal that doesn't occur in this selection. The cell installs; the instruction clears. Nothing is at risk on-screen. Repetition of a warning that never fires reads as padding until it pays off later, and the corpus doesn't show me it paying off.

**SEVERITY 7 — Tonal lurch at the station.**
"(warmth you did not make.)" and Bell's plain-spoken, kindly register ("one left with a clean seal") arrive with no transition from the cold registry counter that had just reduced me to a suit record. The station is simultaneously the machine that owns me and a cozy shop. That contradiction could be the point — but the writing doesn't dramatize the friction, it just moves rooms.

**SEVERITY 8 — "Resolve Organic Samples" / "Attend the relationship" — the verb "attend" is overloaded.**
`Attend Fire Mismatch`, `Attend Living Noise`, `Attend Tree Mismatch` are the auditor's coercive verbs. Then post-freedom: `[F] ATTEND THE POND RESPONSE`, `[F] ATTEND THE RELATIONSHIP`. Reusing the auditor's exact verb for acts of care is either a very fine ironic reclamation or an accident. Nothing in the shipped text tells me which, and after eight hours of the former I read the latter as flattening.

## 4. EMOTIONAL ARC

- **Builds strongly:** the opening four lines through the refusal. Best-constructed stretch in the corpus. Every beat escalates and the payoff ("no.") is earned by the compliance that precedes it.
- **Spike, earned:** the world's response to the refusal — "(water answers first. then wings. then everything at once.)" This is the corpus's high point and it's earned by the preceding silence.
- **Sustained, good:** dive → rebuild → launch. "i came down this line without being asked. i am going back up it on purpose."
- **Sag:** Tidegarden settlement. Ten instruction blocks, three narration lines. The prose is doing the emotional work alone against a wall of fetch verbs.
- **Spike, earned, and the best structural decision here:** the hearth fault. It retroactively justifies the sag.
- **Spike, earned:** the registry counter. "(the suit is accepted before you are.)" is a gut-punch precisely because I earlier refused to give a designation and the query "did not close."
- **Sag / unearned flatness:** the ending. The story spends its second half establishing that the cell is a leash, then the leash is installed with no reaction. The corpus declares the final line absent — but even so, `Install the Sealed Bonded Cell` → instruction clears is a whimper after "carry back what the world could not give."

## 5. LOOSE THREADS

**Setups without payoff (in this selection):**
- W-7744's survival and fault log.
- "UNSCHEDULED HULL · INTEREST RAISED" / "nothing has asked yet. something has started paying attention." Explicit dread, never cashed.
- "the query does not close." An open, unresolved registry query on an undesignated pilot — then I voluntarily walk into that registry's counter and *give them the designation*, and the query is never mentioned again. This is the largest wasted setup in the corpus.
- "HABITAT CORE ONLINE · ADDRESS NOT YET PROVEN." Proven how? By whom? Never revisited.
- The bond warning (see Coherence #6).
- "the pond answered every time i asked. i am leaving anyway — that is what the answers were for." Gorgeous, and the pond/Tidegarden waterline echo is set up ("LIVING WATERLINE") but never closed.
- The tree — "the site gets small. the tree does not. i keep finding it." I *keep finding it*, and then never do again. That line promises recurrence and the corpus delivers none.

**Payoffs without setup:**
- Bell. A named character with a warm voice arrives in the last fifth with no prior existence, and the corpus omits her greeting. She's the only other person in the story and I met her at a counter.
- The Kestrel's fabricator being able to make a habitat core but not a bonded cell. The distinction is asserted at the moment it's needed. I never learned the rule before it was used against me.
- The dive: "(returned with breath. returned with a way to leave.)" — the ship comes out of a pond because a repaired sterilization tool pinged something. That's a lot of plot in one parenthesis.

## 6. THREE LINES TO CUT OR REWRITE FIRST

1. **`[F] CHOOSE THIS DRY, LEVEL SITE.`** (with marker `RECOMMENDED HABITAT SITE · CHOOSE`)
   Cut or replace. This single line refutes the story's central claim. You cannot spend an act teaching me that "the world supplies no order" and then hand me a recommended answer with the word CHOOSE on it. Let me place the foundation anywhere; let the narration comment on where I put it.

2. **`Resolve Organic Samples` (unaccompanied by narration)`**
   Rewrite. This is the moral hinge and it's a euphemism with no consequence. Either give it a lowercase line as devastating as "(the warmth goes out because a voice named it wrong.)", or make it refusable and let me carry the choice. As shipped it's the story flinching from its own best idea.

3. **`WAIT. LISTEN BEFORE ASSIGNING IT A PURPOSE.`**
   Rewrite the *delivery*, not the sentiment. In all-caps standing-instruction voice, this is the audit telling me to be free. Move it into the lowercase interior voice ("i could wait. i could listen before i named it.") or, better, remove the instruction entirely and let the marker read `REPAIRED MAW` with no verb. The corpus already proves silence works — "[The standing instruction clears]" is its most powerful stage direction.

## 7. CHARACTER READ

**W-7743 (me).**
*Wants:* to keep the things that are hers — the dark, the fire, the tree — and later, to keep a home alive.
*Opposed by:* a naming authority that can delete reality by classifying it, and, more interestingly, by material dependency (the bonded cell).
*Tactic:* refusal, then withholding (declining to state a designation), then — crucially — **submission as strategy** (presenting the designation at the counter to get what the hearth needs).
*Belief that changes:* from "if I refuse the naming, I am free" to "I can be free and still need them; home has a supply chain." This arc is genuinely there in the text and it's good. The corpus gives strong evidence via the paired lines "they asked for a designation. what i have is not one" → "DESIGNATION: W-7743 · RECORD ACCEPTED."

**W-7744 (the auditor).**
*Wants:* to restore a "VERIFIED REALITY FLOOR" — to make the site match the model.
*Opposed by:* a witness.
*Tactic:* naming, euphemism, and enforced participation (he makes *me* do the work).
*Belief that changes:* **Insufficient evidence.** He flees; the network logs him as the fault. Whether anything in him changed is unknowable from the shipped text. Given the mirroring setup ("i lived there"), this is a significant miss — he's the story's only antagonist and he exits without an interior.

**Bell (B-7073).**
*Wants:* **Insufficient evidence.** She sells me a component and explains it accurately and kindly.
*Opposed by:* nothing shown.
*Tactic:* plain, careful explanation. Her lowercase register is the only formal signal that she is a person rather than a system — a smart, cheap piece of characterization.
*Belief that changes:* none shown. She is a vending machine with a good voice. The omitted greeting may contain more; as shipped, she is a function.

**The Audit Network / Registry / Issuing Station.**
Reads as a single antagonist that is not malicious but *indifferent and total*. Its best characterization is bureaucratic: it closes files on dead hulls, raises interest on unscheduled ones, and never closes a query it can't answer. Its inconsistency (tracks a hull, not a missing auditor) is either fine satire or an oversight — unresolvable from the surface.

**The world / pond / tree.**
Functions as a character. "(the pond answers in structure, not instruction.)" is a precise and lovely definition of what a non-coercive presence is. *Wants:* nothing — that's the point. It is the counter-example to the audit. The tree in particular is set up as an object of loyalty ("i keep finding it") and then abandoned.

## 9. AGENCY LOG

**Actions I believe the player must take (hard gates):**
- Attend all three deviations (Fire / Living Noise / Tree Mismatch).
- Douse the Fire; Resolve Organic Samples. *(Presented as required; see Coherence #2 — I genuinely can't tell.)*
- `Test Maw on Protected Tree` → refuse. This appears to be a gate that is passed *by refusing*, which is a neat inversion if intentional.
- `[F] RECOVER THE FIELD KIT`, `[F] BEGIN MAW REPAIR`.
- The full Tidegarden build chain: scan waterline → attend relationship → choose site → `[C] CRAFT HABITAT CORE` → `[B] PLACE A FOUNDATION` → `[F] INSTALL HABITAT CORE` → enclose → `[F] CERTIFY THE WORKING SHELTER` → `[F] REST AT THE SECOND HEARTH`.
- `[F] READ THE HEARTH FAULT`; `[F] ATTEMPT TO FABRICATE A REPLACEMENT CELL` (a required *failure* — good design, it makes the player perform the dead end rather than be told about it).
- Board, cross to origin world, land, `[F] REQUEST A SOURCE`, `[F] CLAIM THE BEARING`.
- `HOLD [SPACE] TO IGNITE`; fly and hold the bearing.
- `[F] PRESENT THE SUIT RECORD` → `PRESENT SUIT RECORD` → `DECLARE: ISSUED COMPONENT`. **This is the one required action I actively did not want to take**, which is the corpus's single best piece of agency design.
- `PRESENT HABITAT FAULT` → `ACCEPT ISSUED BONDED CELL`.
- `[F] STOW THE BONDED CELL AND DEPART` → `[F] INSTALL THE SEALED CELL`.

**Events available through waiting / passivity:**
- `HOLD POSITION UNTIL THE MAW REPAIR COMPLETES.`
- `WAIT. LISTEN BEFORE ASSIGNING IT A PURPOSE.`
- `REMAIN WITH THE RESONANCE. / LET THE STRUCTURE SETTLE.`
- `WATCH IT, OR [F] ATTEND THE POND RESPONSE.` — an explicit passive alternative.
- `REST BECOMES AVAILABLE AT NIGHT.` — a time gate.
- `THE SOURCE IS RESOLVING. HOLD.`
The Maw sequence is the only stretch where waiting is dramatized as *meaning* something, and it's the most thematically ambitious passage in the corpus.

**Genuine branch points I could identify:** exactly one — `TEST THE MAW ON STONE OR BASE METAL. / OR REMAIN WITH IT AND [F] LOWER IT TO LISTEN.` And the narration flattens it deliberately: cutting and lowering both produce "(the world supplies no order.)" I understand the intent I *think* is there (the point is that no one judges you), but as a player the effect was that my only real choice was announced as inconsequential.

**Visible fallback / rescue:** none observed. Every marker is directive and persistent, re-issuing on approach (`RETURN TO...`, `REMAIN AT...`). There is no failure state, no timer, no penalty shown anywhere in this selection — including on the degrading hearth cell. The absence of any possible loss is, I suspect, the deepest reason the second half sags.

---

**Overall.** The first act is excellent — a genuinely original horror premise (reality as a naming authority) delivered in two typographic registers with almost no exposition, culminating in a one-word refusal that the world physically answers. The hearth fault is a first-rate structural reversal. The counter scene lands. But the game keeps writing beautiful sentences about self-determination while the HUD tells me where to stand, and it flinches at the one moment — the animals — where it should have made me pay. Fix the imperative voice after the refusal and make compliance cost something, and this is very good.
